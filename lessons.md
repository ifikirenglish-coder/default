## Voice & Tone

- Use "kami" when referring to iFikir English as the brand or team (not "kita"). Drop the pronoun entirely when the sentence reads naturally without it.
- **Avoid "kami" and "our" in campaign copy.** Only use when absolutely necessary. Rephrase around it.
- **Never use "korang".** Audience is 30+ working professionals. Use "anda" or drop the pronoun entirely. "Korang" sounds too young and casual for this audience.
- Never use em dashes (—). Replace with a comma, a new sentence, or just remove the pause. Em dashes are an AI tell.
- Do NOT mirror the live session speaking style in written content. No "pernah rasa macam tu?", no rhetorical check-ins, no "jelas ke?" energy. That style works in live class, not in writing. Written content should be direct, clear, and flowing — not a transcribed monologue.
- Short follow-up pings work in Malaysian WhatsApp culture. Messages like "Cik?", "Cik Joanna?", "Macam mana cik?" feel personal and draw attention. Long follow-up messages get ignored because they look automated and spammy. Do NOT "fix" or replace short ping-style follow-ups with longer value-based messages. This is confirmed from real experience.

## Terminology (Internal vs External Language)

These are internal terms. Never use them in customer-facing copy — audience does not know them:
- "KE" → use "Kuasai English Trial Class" or "iFikir Trial Class"

### Everyday Language Rules (Malaysian Professional — 30+)
Write like a professional talking to a colleague. Not formal, not too casual. Use words people actually say daily:
- "menghentikan" → "stop" (cth: "ada benda yang stop anda")
- "ada sesuatu" → "ada benda"
- "kerana" → "sebab"
- "klik" (in Malay, meaning resonated/clicked into place) → don't use. Rephrase: "bila semuanya jadi clear", "bila anda faham", "bila rasa masuk akal"
- "untuk yang nak teruskan" → weak, no pain. Replace with SIAPA pain: "untuk yang tak sanggup terus gugup speaking masa meeting"
- CTAs like "DM kalau ada soalan" → weak. Use: "Nak join tapi ada soalan? Reply mesej ni." Pull them in with a question first.

## Content Strategy

- Sempena campaigns should include both common occasions (Hari Raya, Merdeka, New Year) AND unusual profession-specific days (Hari Jurutera, Hari Akauntan, Hari Guru). The unusual ones attract attention precisely because nobody else uses them. This is a Marketing Meletup lesson.
- Always structure sales copy, ads, and CTAs using the SIAPA formula: Sasaran → Isu Kritikal → Akibat → Potensi → Action. Never just explain the product — connect problem to solution to result.
- Lead with the pain (Isu + Akibat) before presenting the solution (Potensi). Emotion first, logic second.
- Primary content focus: Solution Aware audience. These people are also Problem Aware — they already feel the pain. Don't over-explain the problem. They're in "nak selesaikan" mode, actively looking for the right solution.
- Solution Aware content = intercept people with clear intent. Match their actual search language: "tengah cari kelas English", "nak improve English cepat", "nak join program", "tengah cari trainer". Be direct, not clever.
- Don't educate Solution Aware audiences about their problem. Position iFikir as the right answer to a decision they've already made.

**TEACH video — winning format (confirmed from performance data 2026-04-06):**

Proven by: Already send (86K), Please advice (42.6K), Please revert (34.4K).

```
0:00–0:03  STATUS THREAT hook — not curiosity, not question
           OK: "Ayat ni buat orang judge English anda."
           NOT OK: "Tu salah ke?" (too soft, curiosity only)

0:04–0:07  Written context: email / WhatsApp kerja
           Name the mistake clearly.

0:08–0:12  Simple logic — NEVER use grammar terms
           No: "present perfect tense"
           Yes: "Bila guna 'already', ia dah lepas. Jadi 'send' jadi 'sent'."

0:13–0:15  RETENTION RESET — mandatory
           "Yang ni ramai tak sedar sebab bunyi dia macam betul."
           This line stops scrolling. Do not skip.

0:16–0:30  2–3 correct alternatives, fast. No explanation between them.

0:31–0:35  Memory hook (optional — boosts saves when used)

0:36–0:42  IDENTITY close — not generic CTA
           OK: "Kalau kerja dengan klien, ayat ni penting."
           OK: "Save ni — esok confirm guna."
           NOT OK: "Follow for more English tips."
```

**What makes written workplace English outperform spoken/polite English:**
- Written = permanent. Can be forwarded. Creates silent professional judgement.
- Spoken politeness feels optional; written errors feel embarrassing.
- Best topics: emails, WhatsApp kerja, meeting follow-ups. NOT casual spoken phrases.

**Topic selection criteria (all 4 must apply):**
1. Grammatically wrong (not just tone preference)
2. Used daily at work
3. Sounds "okay" locally — so people don't know they're wrong
4. Fixable in one line

**Target video length:** 35–45s. Not shorter, not longer.

**Hook must create professional STATUS RISK, not just curiosity.**
Status risk = "orang akan judge", "nampak tak professional", "buat orang salah faham".
Curiosity = "betul ke?", "tahu tak?". Curiosity alone gets 3–5K. Status risk gets 30–80K.
