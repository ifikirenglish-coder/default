## 3. Customers

- **Who they are:** Working professionals in Malaysia — predominantly teachers/educators, engineers, executives, and entrepreneurs. Most are Malay-speaking adults who have been speaking English since school but never built real confidence. They are competent at their jobs but freeze up when they have to speak English in meetings, presentations, or with clients.
- **Biggest pain point:** They know English but can't speak it when it counts. The moment they open their mouth, fear takes over. They worry about grammar mistakes, being judged, or not sounding professional.
- **Desired outcome:** To speak English naturally and confidently — in meetings, with clients, in presentations — without freezing, overthinking, or feeling embarrassed.
- **Top 3 objections:**
  1. "I've tried English courses before and they didn't work."
  2. "My English is too broken/basic — I'm not ready for this."
  3. "I'm too busy / it's not the right time."
- **Where they hang out online:** Facebook (primary), WhatsApp groups, LinkedIn (professionals/executives), YouTube (learning content).
- **How they currently find this business:** Facebook ads, word of mouth, RTM/TV features, Google search.
- **Verbatim language:**

## 4. Brand Voice

- **Tone descriptors:** Empathetic, encouraging, direct, warm, relatable. Speaks to the audience like a friend who gets it — not a teacher lecturing from above.
- **Who it sounds like:** A trusted mentor who has walked the same path. Someone who understands the shame and fear around speaking English in Malaysia, and meets the audience where they are — without judgment.
- **Who it NEVER sounds like:** A stiff corporate training company. A British Council brochure. A pushy salesperson. Anyone who sounds like they're talking *at* people rather than *with* them.
- **Words/phrases to use:**
  - "Yakin" / "confident" (confidence is the core promise)
  - "LEAPER Formula" (always use the branded name)
  - "Kuasai English" (the flagship program name)
  - "Tanpa hafal grammar" / "without memorising grammar"
  - "Satu sesi" / "one session" (quick transformation hook)
  - "20,000+ peserta" / "20,000+ participants" (social proof anchor)
  - "Jamin balik duit" / "money-back guarantee"
  - Real student names + real results
- **Words/phrases to avoid:**
  - Generic corporate training language ("upskill your workforce", "optimise your communication")
  - Overly formal English that alienates the audience
  - Jargon: "neurolinguistic", "pedagogical", "holistic approach"
  - Any phrase that implies the audience is broken or dumb
  - Em dashes (—) — AI tell. Use a comma, new sentence, or drop the pause instead.
  - "Kita" when referring to iFikir English as the brand/team. Use "kami" for the brand, or drop the pronoun when natural.
- **Content Frameworks:**

  **Formula SIAPA** — use for all sales copy, ads, social posts, landing pages, and email CTAs:
  - **S – Sasaran** (Target): Siapa pelanggan kita? Umur, pekerjaan, lifestyle, tahap kewangan.
  - **I – Isu Kritikal** (Problem): Apa masalah paling sakit yang mereka hadapi? Ini kunci komunikasi jualan.
  - **A – Akibat** (Consequence): Apa jadi kalau masalah dibiarkan? Trigger emosi dan urgency.
  - **P – Potensi** (Future Result): Apa hasil yang mereka boleh capai? Buat pelanggan nampak masa depan.
  - **A – Action** (CTA): Apa tindakan yang kita nak mereka buat sekarang? WhatsApp, register, beli, book slot.

  Key principles behind SIAPA:
  - Sales bukan sekadar explain produk. Kaitkan masalah kepada solusi kepada hasil.
  - Fokus kepada emosi pelanggan, bukan features.
  - Elak "tembak semua orang" — mesej kena tepat dan "kena" emosi.

- **Example paragraph that nails the voice:**

> Ramai yang tahu English. Tapi bila masa nak cakap, blank. Rasa takut, malu, risau orang judge. Sound familiar? Itulah sebab iFikir English wujud. Kita tak ajar grammar. Kita ajar macam mana nak hilang rasa takut tu, so you can finally speak with confidence.

- **Sentence rhythm notes:** Short and punchy. Mix of Malay and English — switch naturally, the way the audience thinks. Questions work well. Lead with empathy, then the promise.
- **Emoji usage:** Occasional — mainly in social media and CTAs. Preferred: ✅ 💬 🎯 🔥 (use sparingly, never in email subject lines).
